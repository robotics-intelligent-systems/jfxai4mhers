## Source translation: regulatory context

Regulation of sport hunting and control of weapons and military security infrastructure involve strict legal frameworks, supervisory bodies and administrative procedures.

In Peru, the source describes licensing as a coordinated chain involving SERFOR (wildlife management), SUCAMEC (civilian-use weapons) and the Ministry of Defence / Armed Forces (reserved areas and military equipment).

## Source translation: 1. Sport-hunting and hunting-firearm licences

The source states that hunting requires two mandatory, independent credentials: administrative authorization for hunting and a licence for possession of the firearm.

### A. Sport-hunting licence — SERFOR

Issued by the National Forest and Wildlife Service (SERFOR).

- **Training certificate:** proof of completing and passing an Education, Safety and Ethics in Hunting course delivered by an authorized entity.
- **Application and sworn declaration:** signed application (Form F27), accompanied by a sworn declaration of no criminal record or wildlife-related penalties.
- **Identification:** valid national identity document (DNI) or foreign resident card.
- **Fees:** proof of payment of the processing fee at Banco de la Nación.
- **Validity stated by the source:** 2 years or indefinite, depending on the issuance regime and applicable regulations.

### B. Firearm licence — hunting category L2, SUCAMEC

Administered by the National Superintendency for the Control of Security Services, Arms, Ammunition and Explosives for Civilian Use (SUCAMEC).

- **Prior SERFOR licence:** copy of a valid sport-hunting licence.
- **Psychosomatic health certificate:** approved medical, psychological and psychiatric evaluation at an IPRESS healthcare institution registered with SUSALUD and SUCAMEC.
- **Theory and practical examination:** assessment of firearm handling, disassembly, safety rules and a shooting test at a SUCAMEC range.
- **Background checks:** no criminal, judicial or police records for intentional offences, and no domestic-violence records.
- **Validity stated by the source:** 3 years, renewable.

## Source translation: 2. Military security rules and spatial restrictions

The source states that interactions between sport hunting or aircraft/drone use and military areas are strictly regulated to protect national-security space.

### A. Prohibited and restricted areas

- **Military-unit perimeters:** the source claims an absolute prohibition on hunting, carrying loaded weapons and photography within the security perimeters of air bases, barracks, army forts and naval facilities.
- **Military airspace:** the source states that the Peruvian Air Force (FAP), coordinating with the DGAC, defines restricted airspace where reconnaissance drones or RPAS, including hunting-support use, require express authorization from the area's military command.

### B. Military-only versus civilian equipment and calibres

- **Military calibres:** the source claims that hunting L2 and sport L3 licences expressly prohibit automatic weapons/ammunition or calibres reserved for the Armed Forces or National Police, giving **5.56 × 45 mm NATO** and **7.62 × 51 mm NATO** as examples. These examples are retained as unverified source claims; they are not a verified prohibited-calibre list.
- **Types described as authorized for hunting:** smoothbore shotguns, repeating/bolt-action rifles in calibres permitted for particular wildlife, and high-powered airguns. This source list is not an authorization for a particular item or use.

## Source translation: 3. Comparative requirements matrix

| Domain | Regulatory body | Key requirement stated by the source | Main restriction stated by the source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hunting eligibility | SERFOR | Approved hunting and ethics course | Prohibited in protected natural areas (ANP) without a management plan. |
| Hunting firearm — L2 | SUCAMEC | IPRESS medical certificate and range test | Does not permit carrying the firearm loaded in urban areas. |
| Military security / land | MINDEF / FAP / CCFFAA | Special entry passes / C2 authorization | Complete prohibition on hunting or sensor use in border strips and military premises. |

The source does not define “C2 authorization.” MINDEF denotes the Ministry of Defence; CCFFAA is the source's abbreviation for the Armed Forces Joint Command.

## Editorial review and official references

Review date: 7 October 2026. Official indexed sources were checked for a limited comparison. No complete review of consolidated legislation, current site restrictions or individual eligibility was performed.

| Topic | Finding and verification limit |
| --- | --- |
| SERFOR validity and application | The [official SERFOR application page](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/serfor/pages/9348-solicitar-licencia-de-caza-deportiva) states indefinite validity and identifies Form F27 and hunting education/safety/ethics training. It also includes an alternative for foreign applicants with a valid home-country hunting licence. The source's two-year alternative was not substantiated; its checklist omits alternatives and should not replace the official procedure. |
| Separate permissions | A [SERFOR administrative resolution](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/serfor/normas-legales/963028-0731-2018-minagri-serfor-atffs-lima) distinguishes the hunting licence from authorization for hunting under the approved calendar. Consequently, the source's “two credentials” statement should not be read as an exhaustive list of permissions or as granting unrestricted hunting rights. |
| SUCAMEC category and requirements | [SUCAMEC's published regulation of Law 30299](https://www.sucamec.gob.pe/web/IMAGENES/2019/pdfs/reglamento_ley30299.pdf) identifies L2 hunting licensing and includes an IPRESS psychosomatic certificate among the requirements. This is an older regulatory publication, not a verified consolidation of all subsequent amendments. The source's prior-SERFOR-licence requirement, dual-registration wording, examination details, background-check wording and three-year validity were not fully validated against the current procedure. |
| Possession, use and transport | The source uses possession/carry terminology alongside L2 licensing. These concepts should not be treated as interchangeable. Check the [official SUCAMEC procedures](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/sucamec/informes-publicaciones/1770065-tramites-frecuentes-de-armas-municiones-y-conexos) and applicable rules for the precise licence, ownership and transport requirements. |
| Protected areas, military sites and airspace | The blanket claims about protected natural areas, border strips, photography, sensors and military security perimeters were not verified as universally applicable rules. A management plan or a military authorization alone should not be inferred to satisfy every required permission. The RPAS approval route and “C2 authorization” remain unresolved. |
| Weapons and calibre examples | Neither the two NATO cartridge examples nor the source's allowed-weapon list was verified as a current legal classification. The translation preserves the original examples without establishing that a specific firearm, cartridge or airgun is permitted or prohibited. |
